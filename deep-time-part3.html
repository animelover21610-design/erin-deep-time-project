Progress report draft

For Part 3, I continued my interactive scrolling website using the same full-history scale of one pixel for every million years. My main challenge was keeping all 23 Paleozoic and 14 Mesozoic events readable without changing their correct spacing. Some events share the same course date, so I kept their markers aligned and placed their labels beside the timeline. I added separate zoomed views with clearly labeled uniform scales, kept both endpoints of time ranges such as Pangea, and added the 14 required illustrations plus two extra visuals for each era. My extras are Pangea and flying insects for the Paleozoic, and pterosaurs and modern birds for the Mesozoic.

I was surprised by how little space these two eras occupy compared with the Precambrian. Using the course dates, the Paleozoic lasts 292 million years and the Mesozoic lasts 182 million years. Together, they fill only 474 pixels of my 4,540-pixel main timeline, or about 10.4% of Earth’s history, while the Precambrian fills 4,000 pixels. Seeing so many familiar life forms and major changes concentrated in this much shorter section helped me understand how much time came before them.
