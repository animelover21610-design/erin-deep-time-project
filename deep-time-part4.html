Progress report draft

For Part 4, I completed the Cenozoic section of my interactive scrolling website while keeping my original scale of one pixel for every million years. Based on the earliest life listed in my course spreadsheet, the first prokaryotes appeared 3.5 billion years ago. This means life has existed for about 77.1% of Earth's total history: (3.5 billion / 4.54 billion) x 100. I was surprised that life has been around for so much of Earth's history, while complex and familiar life forms appeared much later. I used separately labeled zoom views to make recent events readable without changing the main timeline's scale.

Homo sapiens appeared about 300,000 years ago. Using Earth's total history again, (300,000 / 4,540,000,000) x 100 is about 0.0066%. Our species fills only the final 0.3 pixel of my 4,540-pixel timeline. Humans did not live alongside non-bird dinosaurs such as T. rex: those dinosaurs went extinct about 66 million years ago, roughly 65.7 million years before Homo sapiens appeared. Birds are living dinosaurs, so humans do coexist with that surviving lineage today. Seeing the full timeline next to the human-history zoom helped me understand how recent our species is.

Sources and course-date notes

Event dates and terminology follow the course sheet. The line “Paleozoic era” in the Part 4 directions appears to be a heading typo: the six named milestones are Cenozoic. Life’s percentage uses the earliest life listed in the course sheet, not an exact known date for the origin of life. Both percentages use Earth’s entire 4.54-billion-year history as the denominator.
