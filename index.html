Scale & sources

The main timeline

Earth's 4.54 billion years occupy a vertical line 4,540 pixels long. The beginning is at the top. The present is at the bottom. Every interval uses the same scale.

1 pixel = 1 million years

How the zoom views work

Each view enlarges its entire time interval by the same amount. The current scale appears above the timeline. The small overview always shows all of Earth's history at one linear scale. Its size changes to fit the screen.

For any view: position = (oldest age in view − event age) × pixels per million years. Margins sit outside the measured timeline. Browser zoom enlarges everything together.

Points, spans, and crowded events

Dots mark event ages. Vertical bars show both endpoints of a date range. Bars continue across era boundaries when the course dates do. Their visible portions clip at a zoom view's edge. Group labels collect nearby events without moving their dots. Use the event selector or the full index to reach every event.

Modern dates fall within a fraction of a pixel on the full timeline. Their coordinates retain that precision. Choose Human history or My birthday to see them at a readable scale.

Your birthday

December 10, 1998 is 10,151 days before the fixed present, September 25, 2026. Dividing by 365.2425 days per year gives approximately 27.792494 years, or 0.027792494 kya. Its full-timeline position is 4,539.999972207506 pixels from Earth's formation.
